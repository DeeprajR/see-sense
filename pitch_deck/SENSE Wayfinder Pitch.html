02 · The problem
Every new room is a puzzle
A cane finds what's at your feet, not where the door is
GPS doesn't work indoors
Description apps say "there's a door", not how to reach it
A phone needs a free hand, and the cane already has one
03 · Who it's for
Blind and low-vision people who get around on their own
Independent
They already move on their own with a white cane
Indoors, unfamiliar
Colleges, offices, hospitals, stations, shops
One moment
"Where is it, and how do I get there?"
04 · The solution
A guide that looks, plans and walks you there
1
Hold the button
"Take me to the exit"
2
It looks around
"Turn right about 90 degrees." … "OK, stop."
3
It plans the walkway
"Walk about 3 steps. The tables will be on your left."
4
It warns only when it matters
"A person on your left, coming toward you."
5
It clears the way
"The chair is right in front of you. Push it to your left."
6
You arrive
"The door is right in front of you. The handle is on the right."
05 · How it works
Claude sees. JEV decides fast. You hear only what matters.
Part
Job
Head camera
Claude's eyes; also measures your turn, to about 1°
Claude Sonnet 5.5
Understands the room, picks the nearest door, plans the route
JEV (TypeSafe)
In 0.3 s: request, command or misheard? What needs a warning?
Button + Vosk
Hold to speak, press for the next step; speech recognised offline
Earbuds
Every instruction in a natural voice, stored on the device
Live view
A helper, trainer or judge sees what Wayfinder sees, in any browser
06 · Design principles
Built around how blind people actually move
Quiet by default
Only instructions and real warnings are spoken
Survey before planning
Finds the nearest door, and checks another angle before calling the way blocked
Hands free
One button, no phone in hand
Honest
Says when it's unsure; never says a path is "safe"
Respectful
Never asks you to move a person or someone's wheelchair
07 · What it's built from
